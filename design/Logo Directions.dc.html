ancora · Logo round 3

Vault, refined.

Three adjustments of 3a. All fix the cramped arms and give the anchor a longer shank; the shield is now a true geometric form (straight sides, a single bottom arc). They differ in how the star and shield meet.

4a Cut All negative space

Flat top, straight sides, one arc: the shield as a vault door rather than a heraldic crest. Star and anchor are both cut from it. One colour, works anywhere.

4b Crest Star breaks the edge · recommended

The starlight star sits on the shield’s top edge, half in, half out, with a hairline of ground around it. The star is the only light element: it is what you notice first, and it keeps the two-colour rhythm of brass + starlight from the rest of the brand.

4c Badge Rounded, softer

Rounded top corners soften the shield so it matches the app’s rounded surfaces and pill buttons. Friendlier, slightly less “security badge”, same cut-out anchor.

ancora · Logo round 2

Solid, not drawn.

Round 1 used outlines, which read as interface icons. These are filled shapes with weight, the way fintech marks are built: one silhouette you could stamp on a card. The star is cut into or grown out of the anchor rather than placed on top of it.

3a Vault Star cut from a shield

A brass shield with the anchor and star cut out of it. Shield says custody and rules; the negative-space anchor is quiet, not nautical. Reads as a security badge next to Revolut or Qonto.

3b Monogram The “a” is the anchor

A heavy single-storey “a” with a star above it, like a dotted letter. The bowl is the anchor’s weight, the stem its shank. It is the first letter of the wordmark, so mark and name are one system. Closest to how neobanks brand today.

3c Plumb Weight on a line

An anchor reduced to its meaning: a star, a short line, and a heavy weight that sits flat on the ground. The weight carries a slot, like a coin slot or a ledger line. Abstract, calm, no flukes to misread.

ancora · Logo round 1

Anchor, with a star for a ring.

Three ways to draw the same idea. All share one stroke weight, no stock, no rope, and a 4-point star that doubles as the ring. Each shown large, as an app icon, and at 32 and 16 px on both grounds.

2a Held Arms as one arc

The arms become a single bowl holding the shank, like a cradle. Short flukes turn outward, not up, so they don’t read as arrows. The small cuts drop the flukes entirely: star, line, bowl.

2b Set Star grown from the shank

A curved four-point star (a sparkle, the shape of Stellar’s own vocabulary) sits closer to the shank so the gap reads as intentional. Wide arms with short upward tips instead of flukes: a wider, more planted stance.

2c Keel Solid, one piece

The arms curl back into two closed loops, like a keel seen end-on or a mooring cleat. No flukes at all, so nothing points. The most ownable silhouette; the one that looks least like a stock anchor icon.
